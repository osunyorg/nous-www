The article revisits the drafting of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, challenging accounts that portray its origins as either a universal consensus or an imposition of liberal Western values. It argues that the Declaration contains an ambiguous understanding of rights that resists both interpretations. Recognising this ambiguity opens possibilities for understanding human rights as an ongoing political struggle over legitimate authority and the meaning of humanity.

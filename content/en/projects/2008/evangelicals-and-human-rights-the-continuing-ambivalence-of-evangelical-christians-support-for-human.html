The article examines the complex relationship between American evangelical Christianity and international human rights. It argues that evangelical beliefs have encouraged engagement with certain human rights issues, particularly religious freedom, while theological priorities such as evangelism and individual salvation have limited broader support for human rights. Nichols also considers how growing involvement in humanitarian organisations and greater emphasis on human dignity might encourage closer engagement with the human rights movement.

Modirzadeh examines the gap between TWAIL's ambition to transform international law and its practical influence on Global South political agency. While acknowledging its contributions to exposing colonial structures, she argues that TWAIL has struggled to develop coherent strategies for change. She identifies unresolved questions concerning state power, political violence and representation, and proposes alternative directions for translating critical scholarship into collective action.

Lloredo Alix argues that recognising the rights of nature requires rethinking conventional legal understandings of rights. Drawing on post-humanist, anti-capitalist and postcolonial perspectives, he challenges approaches centred on human interests, individual ownership and European legal traditions. The article presents natural entities as potential rights-holders and considers how ecological understandings of rights may transform legal relationships between humans and the natural world.

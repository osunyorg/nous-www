The article examines how deliberate misinformation spread by politicians and state officials can undermine access to information, political participation, and public trust. Drawing on European human rights case law, it considers whether existing legal protections could support a right to truthful information from political leaders. It also argues that human rights law needs a broader understanding of harm to address the long-term effects of political disinformation on individuals and democratic debate.

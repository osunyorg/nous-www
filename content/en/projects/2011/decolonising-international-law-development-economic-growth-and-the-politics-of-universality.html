Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2011. summary: >-

Pahuja examines how international law has shaped development policies and relations between wealthy and poorer countries. Through examples involving decolonisation, natural resources and foreign investment, she argues that international law's promises of equality have often been redirected towards economic models that preserve existing inequalities. The book questions how ideas presented as universal can reinforce unequal power relations and considers the possibilities for challenging them.

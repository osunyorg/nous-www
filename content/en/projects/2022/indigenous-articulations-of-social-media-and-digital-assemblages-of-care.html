Examining Indigenous Australians' everyday use of social media, the article explores how digital networks enable communities to organise care, safety, and mutual support. Challenging research that treats Indigenous people primarily as recipients of formal health services, the authors show how users actively identify needs and create relationships of assistance through online connections. It presents these practices as alternative spaces of collective well-being that challenge dominant assumptions about Indigenous digital life.

This article examines how international law shapes everyday life, including through institutions, policies and practices that may reproduce colonial inequalities. Eslava and Pahuja argue that understanding international law requires looking beyond treaties and courts to its effects on people and communities. The article also considers how everyday forms of resistance can challenge existing power relations and contribute to developing a more inclusive international legal order.

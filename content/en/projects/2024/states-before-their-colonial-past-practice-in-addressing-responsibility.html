Marconi examines how former colonial powers have responded to demands for accountability for historical injustices. She identifies a tendency to acknowledge moral or historical responsibility through apologies, symbolic measures and negotiated arrangements while avoiding recognition of legal obligations. The article considers whether these evolving but inconsistent state practices could contribute to future international standards on colonial reparations.

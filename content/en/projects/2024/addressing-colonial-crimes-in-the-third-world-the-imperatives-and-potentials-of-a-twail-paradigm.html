Iyi and Mushoriwa examine whether contemporary international law can adequately address atrocities committed during colonial rule. They focus particularly on the intertemporal rule, which complicates legal accountability for historical crimes. Drawing on TWAIL, they explore alternative approaches to international criminal justice that take demands for accountability and redress from formerly colonised communities more seriously.

Ramasubramanyam combines TWAIL and archival research to challenge Eurocentric accounts of international refugee law. Focusing on India's refugee-hosting practices and its resistance to dominant international legal frameworks, he explores alternative histories and understandings of refugee protection. The article presents archival research as a means of recovering perspectives marginalised in conventional legal histories and reconsidering how international refugee law is studied.

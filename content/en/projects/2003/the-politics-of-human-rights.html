The article challenges the tendency to view politics mainly as a source of corruption or manipulation in human rights. While recognising how powerful economic and political actors can use human rights to advance their interests, Gready argues that rights can also enable resistance, collective action, and social transformation. He proposes a more constructive understanding of human rights politics that acknowledges unequal power while preserving opportunities for political agency, contestation, and change.

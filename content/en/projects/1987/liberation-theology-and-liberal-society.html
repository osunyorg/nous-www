Goulet examines liberation theology as a response to economic exploitation, political oppression and structural injustice in Latin America. He traces its development through religious thought and grassroots struggles, highlighting how collective action informs theological reflection. The essay also critiques inequalities associated with liberal capitalism and presents liberation theology as a means of connecting Christian faith with social transformation and the defence of human rights.

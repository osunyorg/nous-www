Bringing together mainstream and critical scholars, this collection examines the achievements, limitations and possible futures of international human rights. Contributors debate what makes human rights protection effective, why progress faces resistance and whether established approaches remain adequate in a changing political landscape. The book presents competing perspectives on the conditions, strategies and institutions that may shape the future of human rights.

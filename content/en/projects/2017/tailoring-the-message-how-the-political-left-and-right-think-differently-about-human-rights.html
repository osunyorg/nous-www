Braun and Arves examine how political views influence people's responses to human rights messages. Based on experiments with US participants, they found that personal accounts of suffering were more effective among liberal respondents, while graphic images of abuse had a greater impact on conservative respondents. Their findings suggest that human rights organisations need to consider their audiences when communicating about rights violations and encouraging public support.

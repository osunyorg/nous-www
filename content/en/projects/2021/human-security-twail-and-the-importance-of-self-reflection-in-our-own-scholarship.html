Gilder revisits his earlier work on human security through TWAIL scholarship, examining whether people-centred approaches can reproduce the colonial assumptions of international law. He argues that human security should prioritise communities' own understandings of their needs and enable their participation in decisions affecting them. The article also emphasises scholarly self-reflection as a way to challenge dominant assumptions in international legal research and teaching.

Examines tensions between the individual-rights framework established after the Second World War and the collective demands of African anticolonial movements. Drawing on struggles in Algeria and Cameroon, the article questions the universalism of human rights and the authority of former colonial powers in shaping them. It considers Indigenous knowledge and practices as alternative paths towards meaningful liberation.

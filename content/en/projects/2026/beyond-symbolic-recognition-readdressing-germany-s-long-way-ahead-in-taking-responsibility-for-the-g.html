Girardini, Stockinger and Michel examine Germany's response to the colonial genocide in Namibia, focusing on reparations, remembrance and education. They challenge Germany's reliance on historical legal standards to dispute compensation obligations and highlight the exclusion of affected communities from negotiations. The authors argue that meaningful transitional justice requires more than symbolic recognition, including reparations, public education and community participation.

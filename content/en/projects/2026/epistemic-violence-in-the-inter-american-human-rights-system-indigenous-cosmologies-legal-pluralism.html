Carvalho de Mello examines how the Inter-American Human Rights System can marginalise Indigenous knowledge despite advances in legal recognition. Drawing on Brazilian cases and Indigenous legal philosophies, he identifies tensions between Western legal categories and relational understandings of land, nature and community. The article calls for greater recognition of Indigenous legal systems and reforms to how human rights institutions interpret evidence and administer justice.

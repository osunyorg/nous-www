Comparing rights-of-nature frameworks across six countries, Arreaga-Vicuña and colleagues identify constitutional, legislative and judicial approaches to recognising nature as a rights-holder. They find that legal recognition alone is insufficient without effective guardianship, access to courts and enforceable remedies. The review highlights the importance of institutional capacity, Indigenous perspectives and community participation in turning rights-of-nature principles into practice.

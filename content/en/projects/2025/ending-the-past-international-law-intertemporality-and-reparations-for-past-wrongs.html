Fuhrmann and Schweizer challenge the assumption that intertemporal law necessarily prevents reparations for historical injustices. Drawing on international judicial decisions, state practice and legal scholarship, they identify more dynamic interpretations of the doctrine that recognise the relationship between past wrongs and continuing harm. The article considers how these interpretations could support claims for reparations relating to colonialism and slavery.

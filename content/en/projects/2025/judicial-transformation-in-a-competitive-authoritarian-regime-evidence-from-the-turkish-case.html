The article examines how weaknesses in Türkiye's judicial system contributed to democratic backsliding. Esen argues that the political involvement of courts aligned with the secular establishment allowed the AKP government to portray judicial reforms as necessary for democracy, while gradually increasing its control over the judiciary. The study shows how conflicts between courts and elected governments can be used to justify institutional changes that ultimately weaken judicial independence and democratic accountability.

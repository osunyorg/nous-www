Cusato examines how international law's understanding of time shapes claims for colonial and climate reparations. She argues that legal concepts of responsibility, causation and remedies reflect linear and abstract notions of time that can obscure connections between historical injustices and continuing harm. The article explores how reparations claims challenge these assumptions by linking unequal pasts, presents and futures.

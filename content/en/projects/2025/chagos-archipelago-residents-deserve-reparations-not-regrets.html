Originally published in Mail & Guardian. summary: >-

Ngari and Baldwin critique the 2025 UK–Mauritius Chagos Agreement for prioritising territorial sovereignty while leaving key Chagossian rights unresolved. They highlight restrictions on return, inadequate guarantees of compensation and limited community participation in future governance. The authors argue that addressing colonial displacement requires meaningful reparations and the involvement of affected communities, rather than symbolic recognition alone.

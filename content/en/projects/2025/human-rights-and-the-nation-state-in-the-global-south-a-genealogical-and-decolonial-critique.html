Woldeyes examines how international human rights law relies on nation-states for the recognition and protection of rights, despite the colonial origins of state institutions across much of the Global South. Drawing on African experiences, he argues that this arrangement can obscure violence carried out by states themselves. The article proposes rethinking human rights beyond state-centred frameworks to make them more meaningful for marginalised communities.

Tzouvala revisits longstanding criticism of international law's tendency to focus on major crises. Examining scholarly responses to the war in Gaza, she argues that disagreements now extend beyond how international law should respond to violence to whether particular events are recognised as crises at all. The article explores how these divisions challenge shared assumptions within the discipline.

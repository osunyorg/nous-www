Across Africa, young people, women, artists, farmers, and community organisers are challenging traditional ideas about who should shape democracy. Eze describes how these groups are creating new forms of political participation and demanding greater accountability, economic justice, and dignity. Inspired by the African philosophy of ubuntu, he presents a vision of democracy based on shared responsibility, collective well-being, and stronger citizen involvement in decisions that shape their futures.

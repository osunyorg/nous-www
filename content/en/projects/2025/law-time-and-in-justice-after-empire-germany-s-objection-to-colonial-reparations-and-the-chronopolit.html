Graf examines how Germany has invoked international law's rules on non-retroactivity to resist reparations claims arising from its colonial genocide in Namibia. Drawing on parliamentary records, she develops the concept of chronopolitics of deflection to show how legal arguments about time can redirect demands for reparations towards development assistance, limiting recognition of historical responsibility and continuing injustice.

This handbook brings together scholarship on Third World Approaches to International Law (TWAIL), examining how colonial histories and global inequalities continue to shape international legal rules and institutions. Its chapters address human rights, development, economic relations, climate change and other areas of international law. The collection explores how perspectives from the Global South can challenge established legal assumptions and contribute to alternative approaches to international law and global justice.

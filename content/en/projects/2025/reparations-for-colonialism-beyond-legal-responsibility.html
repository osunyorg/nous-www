Ratner questions whether establishing legal responsibility should be the main focus of efforts to secure colonial reparations. He proposes a more flexible role for international law, supporting negotiations and agreements that respond to historical injustices without necessarily requiring recognition of a legal duty to compensate. The article considers how such processes could develop practical standards for future reparations arrangements.
